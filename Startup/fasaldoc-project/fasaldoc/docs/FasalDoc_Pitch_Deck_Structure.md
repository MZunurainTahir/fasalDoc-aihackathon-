# FasalDoc — HATCH Pitch Deck Structure
### Slide-by-slide guide (aim for 10-12 slides, 5-8 min pitch + demo)

General rule: **one idea per slide, minimal text, let you talk.** Judges read decks fast — the deck should support your voice, not replace it.

---

## Slide 1 — Title
- FasalDoc logo/name
- One-line tagline: *"Kheti ka Doctor, Aapki Jeb Mein" / "AI health companion for crops and livestock"*
- Team name, HATCH cohort/date
- Keep it clean — this slide is on screen while you're still walking up, don't overload it.

## Slide 2 — The Problem
- 2-3 sharp stats, not paragraphs. Suggested framing:
  - % of crop yield lost annually to preventable disease/pests in Pakistan
  - % of labor force dependent on agriculture, with minimal access to agronomists/vets
  - The real pain point in one sentence: *"A farmer sees a sick plant and has no fast, reliable, affordable way to know what's wrong or what to do."*
- If you have a real quote from your farmer interviews, use it here — a genuine quote lands harder than any stat.

## Slide 3 — Why Now / Why This Gap Exists
- Smartphone penetration in rural Pakistan has crossed a threshold — cameras are now in farmers' pockets.
- Existing tools (Plantix etc.) exist globally but aren't tuned to Pakistani crops, disease strains, language, or locally available remedies.
- This is the "why hasn't someone done this already" slide — answer it directly, don't let judges wonder.

## Slide 4 — The Solution
- FasalDoc in one sentence + a simple 3-step visual: **Snap a photo → Get instant diagnosis → Get a local remedy**
- Mention both crop and livestock coverage, and note offline capability as a differentiator.
- Keep this slide visual (mockup screenshots), not a wall of bullet points.

## Slide 5 — Live/Recorded Demo
- This is your most important slide. Actually show the app working.
- **Always have a recorded backup video** in case live demo fails (wifi, device issues) — judges have seen too many failed live demos, a smooth recorded fallback is respected, not penalized.
- Show the full flow: camera → diagnosis → remedy card, ideally with a real or realistic test image.

## Slide 6 — Validation / Traction
- Whatever you have, even if small: number of farmers interviewed, pilot users, model accuracy on real-world test images, any testimonial or quote.
- This slide matters *more* than people expect — it's proof you talked to real users, not just built in a vacuum.
- If pilot hasn't launched yet at pitch time, show interview insights and a clear "validated the problem, next step is X" narrative — honesty here builds more trust than inflated claims.

## Slide 7 — Market Size
- Keep it simple, three levels:
  - **TAM:** Total farmers in Pakistan (~8 million+ farm households)
  - **SAM:** Smartphone-owning farmers/agri-workers in your target regions/crops
  - **SOM:** Realistic 1-2 year target (e.g. X districts, Y farmers)
- Don't inflate — judges can tell, and a credible narrower number beats an inflated "billion dollar market" claim with no path to it.

## Slide 8 — Business Model
- How this actually makes money, in one slide: freemium + B2B2C partnerships (agri/fertilizer companies) + marketplace commission (later) + potential government/NGO contracts.
- Be honest about timeline — year 1 is about adoption and data, monetization ramps after.

## Slide 9 — Competitive Landscape
- A simple 2x2 or comparison table: Plantix / PlantVillage Nuru / FasalDoc
- Your edge: Pakistan-specific data, local language, local remedy availability, dual crop+livestock coverage, offline-first design.

## Slide 10 — Roadmap
- Visual timeline: Phase 1 (MVP, current) → Phase 2 (livestock + offline + more crops) → Phase 3 (soil advisory, marketplace, outbreak mapping)
- Shows judges you know what's next and have sequenced it sensibly, not trying to boil the ocean at once.

## Slide 11 — Team
- Photos + one-line role for each of the 5 of you (ML, Mobile, Backend/DevOps, Product/Business, Data/Domain)
- Mention any advisor (agronomist/vet) if secured — this adds real credibility.
- Briefly note relevant skills/experience, even coursework or personal projects — judges want to know you can actually execute.

## Slide 12 — The Ask
- What are you asking HATCH for specifically: incubation support, funding amount (if applicable), mentorship, lab/data access, or introductions to agri partners.
- End with a strong, confident closing line — not a question, a statement of intent: *"We're building this whether we win or not — we'd love HATCH's support to get there faster."*

---

## Delivery Notes

- **Practice the exact time limit** — most HATCH-style pitches are strict on time, and running over hurts more than any slide content.
- **Assign one speaker or a clean handoff** — don't have all 5 people talk unless the format specifically calls for it; a single confident voice (or 2 max) reads stronger than a relay race.
- **Anticipate the hard questions** and rehearse answers as a team:
  - "How is this different from Plantix?"
  - "What happens if your model gives a wrong diagnosis and a farmer loses their crop?"
  - "How will farmers actually discover and trust this app?"
  - "What's your realistic revenue in year 1?"
- **Lead with the demo energy, not the slide count.** A judge remembers a working product far more than a polished deck with no demo.

---

## What NOT to do
- Don't claim "no competitors" — Plantix exists globally, acknowledging it and explaining your edge is more credible than pretending it doesn't.
- Don't inflate market size or user numbers — it erodes trust the moment a judge asks a follow-up question.
- Don't cram every feature from Phase 3 into the pitch — focus judges on what's real *today*, with a credible next-step story.
